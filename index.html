One method. Three fields.

A formal statement can be perfectly correct and still say the wrong thing. The machine that checks it cannot tell the difference.

  1. Mathematics

    Formal proofs of mathematical results, checked one statement at a time. This is where the method was built, and it is what we do today.

  2. Software

    The same check applied to code. Whether a program's specification says what the program was actually meant to do.

  3. Quantum

    Quantum algorithms and protocols, where testing your way to confidence is not an option and proof has to carry the weight.

The kernel checks proofs.
Nobody checks statements.

A formal statement can compile and still say the wrong thing. We read the references the field scores itself against, one statement at a time, and verified every flag by hand.

  1. Benchmark audits

    1,347 statements across three corpora, 76 confirmed defects. All three filings are public: miniF2F, miniF2F v2 and ProofNet#.

  2. Cross-system audits

    Four open autoformalizers, the same 488 problems, each with its own prompts. Failure rates ran from 21.7% to 55.5%. A compile check catches none of it.
